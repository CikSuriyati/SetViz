01

What SetViz is

SetViz is a teaching aid for introductory statistics and discrete mathematics. You pick a set operation and the Venn diagram shades exactly the region that operation describes — so the notation and the picture are always side by side.

It is built for the moment a student can recite that ∩ means "and" but still shades the wrong region. Seeing (A ∩ B)′ light up is more convincing than being told what it means.

Who it is for

Students meeting set notation for the first time, and teachers who want something to project and click through in front of a class. No account, no installation — it runs in a browser and keeps working offline once loaded.

02

What it teaches

Seven stages, each building on the one before. You can take them in order or jump straight to the one you need.

#StageWhat it covers
00 Basics What a set is, the universal set as the surrounding box, and the empty set.
01 Union & Intersection A ∪ BA ∩ B — reading ∪ as "or" and ∩ as "and".
02 Complement & Difference A′A − BA Δ B(A ∩ B)′
03 Subset & Disjoint How the same operation changes meaning as the arrangement changes.
04 Three Sets Eight regions, and working brackets first — A ∩ (B ∪ C)
05 De Morgan's Laws Both identities proved visually, side by side.
06 Quiz Self-check with instant feedback, score and streak.

03

What's in it

Region-accurate shading

Every operation is composed from the individual regions of the diagram, so A − B produces a true crescent with the overlap cleanly removed — not an approximation drawn on top.

Two- and three-set diagrams

Most stages use two circles and four regions. The three-set stage opens up all eight, including bracketed expressions.

Change how the sets sit

Hold an operation fixed and switch between overlapping, B ⊂ A, disjoint and A = B. The notation stays put while the shaded region changes — the point students usually miss.

Try it with real numbers

Enter your own sets and the elements drop into their correct regions as numbered dots, with the result listed in set notation and n(A∪B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A∩B) evaluated using your own values.

De Morgan's Laws, proved

Both identities shown as two diagrams side by side that shade identically — which is what "equal" means for sets.

A quiz that marks properly

Shade a named region or name a shaded one. Marking is per region: green for correct, red for shaded by mistake, faint red for missed. Score and streak are tracked, and a hard mode switches to three-set questions.

Light and dark themes

Follows your system setting and remembers your choice. Arrow keys move between stages, which helps when projecting.

A standing symbol reference

The eight symbols used throughout stay visible beside the diagram, so nobody has to remember what Δ meant three stages ago.

04

How to use it

1

Pick a stage

Use the row of tabs across the top, the Back and Next buttons, or the arrow keys.

2

Click an operation

The diagram shades immediately, and a plain-English explanation appears underneath.

3

Predict before you click

Especially with a class. Being wrong about (A ∩ B)′ once is worth more than being told twice.

4

Turn on real numbers

Put your own values in and watch every figure update as an element moves in or out of the overlap.

5

Finish at the quiz

Nothing is graded or recorded, so wrong answers cost nothing.

The user manual walks through every part of the interface in detail, and includes a suggested lesson route.

05

Project history

SetViz began in October 2025 as a single-page teaching aid and has been developed openly ever since. Every version is public, tagged and dated in the project's repository.

Version 1.0 · October 2025 – August 2026

The first release established the approach that still defines the tool: a student picks an operation, and the diagram shades exactly the region it describes, with a teaching note alongside. It covered seven stages, from the universal set through to De Morgan's Laws, and gained a separate user manual in April 2026.

Silver medal · DIIID 2026

This is the version entered into the DIIID 2026 innovation competition, and the build described in the accompanying extended abstract. It was released publicly under a CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 licence in August 2026.

Version 2.0 · September 2026

A substantial rework of both the interface and the teaching content, built on a new foundation: every operation is now defined as a plain true-or-false rule over the individual regions of the diagram. That one change is what made the rest possible.

  • Three-set diagrams, with all eight regions
  • Switching how the sets sit, independently of the operation
  • Real numbers placed into their regions, with the counting formula evaluated live
  • De Morgan's Laws proved side by side
  • A quiz with per-region marking and a three-set hard mode
  • Seven stages that build on one another, a redesigned interface, and a rewritten manual

The original published address still resolves, so links printed before this release continue to work.
